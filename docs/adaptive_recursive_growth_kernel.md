# Adaptive state-space kernel growth solver

The adaptive recursive solver approximates an investment policy in state space
rather than approximating a time path. It retains the full continuous-time state

\[
\dot z=gz,\qquad \dot k=i(z,k)-\delta k
\]

and uses the homothetic coordinate $q=\log(k/z)$ only as the kernel input. With

\[
f(z,k)=z(k/z)^\alpha,\qquad c(z,k)=f(z,k)-i(z,k),
\]

the residual evaluated along the integrated trajectory is

\[
\nabla c(z,k)^\top
\begin{bmatrix}gz\\i(z,k)-\delta k\end{bmatrix}
-\frac{c(z,k)}{\sigma}\left(f_k(z,k)-\delta-\rho\right)=0.
\]

The implementation obtains the directional derivative of consumption with
`jax.jvp`. Consequently, the LM Jacobian differentiates through both the policy
derivative and every state generated by the numerical integrator.

## RKHS representation

One RBF expansion represents the investment policy:

\[
h(q)=\sum_j\alpha_jK(q,Q_j).
\]

There is one coefficient per accepted state and no separate derivative
approximation. When the Euler equation needs a state derivative of investment,
`jax.jvp` differentiates this same kernel policy.

The code factors $K+\epsilon I=LL^\top$ in float64 and optimizes whitened
coordinates

\[
\beta=L^\top\alpha.
\]

Thus the identity metric seen by LM is exactly the shifted RKHS metric in the
original coefficient coordinates. Residual simulation may independently use
float32 or float64.

## Moving centers

The adaptive integrator has a maximum attempt budget. It returns the initial
state and its accepted internal steps as a fixed-capacity buffer plus an active
mask. Rejected attempts are omitted, and inactive padding contributes neither
residual rows, kernel features, nor RKHS metric entries. Thus the integrator
chooses the actual number and locations of points without changing compiled
array shapes.

The default solve trains and tests over $t\in[0,100]$ and checks the TVC tail
over $t\in[100,200]$. There is no maximum timestep: once the trajectory is near
its fixed point, the controller is free to cross the nearly stationary tail in
large steps. The controller only limits step-size growth to a factor of three
per accepted step. This avoids an uninformative cold-start Jacobian when the
initial stationary trial policy has essentially zero primal integration error,
without forcing redundant points into the long-run tail.

During an LM step, the basis centers and Cholesky factor are fixed solver data,
but the current policy is integrated inside the residual. The accepted states
and their derivatives with respect to policy coefficients are therefore
current. After each LM update, the callback:

1. integrates the policy retained by LM;
2. uses only the integrator's accepted internal states as proposed centers;
3. rebuilds the masked kernel Gram matrix in float64; and
4. transfers the policy values into the new whitened basis.

Refreshing stops when the active count and center displacement stabilize,
making convergence a joint fixed point of the policy and its adaptive mesh. TVC
and saddle-path conditions do not enter the residual or callback; they are
checked after the solve.

Tinydiffeq freezes the nondifferentiable timestep-controller decisions while
differentiating every accepted state update. A maximum-size padded buffer keeps
the kernel and residual dimensions static even as the accepted point count
changes.

The moving simulated grid is related to the simulation-and-projection strategy
of Judd, Maliar, and Maliar (2015), although here the trajectory is differentiated
inside every residual and the basis is refreshed after accepted LM steps:
<https://doi.org/10.3982/QE364>.
